    Clearing Your Record in Los Angeles

    Most people carrying an old California conviction were never told the law changed. Reductions, dismissals and sealing have widened repeatedly over the last decade, and much of it applies backwards. What a background check says about you in six months is, more often than people believe, a decision rather than a fact.

    What you’re facing

    The worry, and what we do about it

    These are the things people actually say on the first call. Each one has an answer, and none of them require you to have decided anything yet.

    One mistake still follows me everywhere.

    It does not have to be permanent. Dismissal under 1203.4, reduction under 17(b) and sealing under 851.91 each change a different part of the record — and which applies depends on your docket, not on how long ago it felt.

    It’s an old case. Is it too late?

    There is no filing deadline for 17(b) reduction or 1203.4 dismissal. We have petitioned successfully on cases more than a decade closed. Age of the conviction is usually an argument in your favor, not an obstacle.

    Every background check surfaces it.

    What a check shows depends on what the record says now. A dismissed conviction, a reduced felony and a sealed arrest each read differently to an employer, a landlord and a licensing board — and each is a separate petition.

    I’m accused of violating probation.

    A violation hearing is not a new trial, and the standard is lower — but it is a hearing, with evidence, argument and alternatives to custody. Many violations resolve with modified terms rather than revocation.

    I want off probation early.

    Penal Code 1203.3 allows a court to terminate probation early, and early termination is frequently the gateway to everything else — because dismissal usually follows the end of probation, not the end of the sentence.

    First hours

    Common mistakes people make early

    Assuming the record is permanentPeople carry convictions that became reducible or dismissible years ago and were never notified. Eligibility law has widened repeatedly — it is worth checking.
    Relying on automatic relief aloneNewer laws clear some records by operation of law, but coverage is incomplete and databases lag. A petition produces a court order you can point to.
    Ignoring an old bench warrantA forgotten warrant blocks relief and compounds quietly. Most can be recalled with counsel, often without you appearing in custody.
    Answering job applications wronglyAfter a 1203.4 dismissal most private employers may not ask about or use it — but the answer differs for licensing and government roles. Getting this right matters.
    Filing in the wrong orderReduce the wobbler first, then dismiss. Done backwards, the relief obtained is narrower than the relief that was available.
    Assuming dismissal means erasure1203.4 dismisses; it does not delete. Knowing precisely what remains visible is what lets you answer questions confidently and truthfully.

    None of these petitions expire — but the job, the lease or the license you need them for usually does.

    How these cases are fought

    Relief is granted on a record, not a form. These are the places these petitions are actually won.

    01 Reading the docket FOUNDATION Eligibility is decided by documents, not memory. ↗

    What the plea actually admitted, what the sentence actually was, whether probation terminated successfully — these details decide which petitions are available, and people routinely misremember them in both directions.

    02 Order of operations STRATEGY Reduce first, then dismiss, then seal. ↗

    A wobbler reduced under 17(b) and then dismissed under 1203.4 produces a materially better record than dismissal alone. Filed in the wrong order, the outcome is narrower than it needed to be.

    03 The rehabilitation showing EVIDENCE Discretionary relief is granted on a life, not a form. ↗

    Employment, education, treatment, service, letters, time since the offense — assembled properly, they are the difference in every discretionary petition and in Romero-adjacent arguments.

    04 Probation, ended early TIMING Dismissal usually waits for probation to end. ↗

    PC 1203.3 early termination is often the unlock for everything downstream, and courts grant it where the terms have been met and there is a reason that matters.

    05 Violation hearings PROCEDURE A violation is a hearing, not a foregone conclusion. ↗

    The standard is lower than trial, but evidence is still tested and alternatives to revocation exist — modified terms, extended time, treatment. Many violations resolve without custody.

    06 Retroactive reclassification LEGISLATION The law has moved, repeatedly, in your favor. ↗

    Prop 47, Prop 64 and later reforms reclassified whole categories and apply backwards. Convictions that were felonies when entered may be misdemeanours today, and petitions exist to say so.

    07 Immigration-aware relief CONSEQUENCE Not all relief helps every purpose. ↗

    1203.4 does not undo an immigration consequence, and vacatur under PC 1473.7 exists for exactly that problem. Choosing the right instrument for the actual goal is the whole job.

    The statute, element by element

    The elements, presumptions and instruction references — folded so the page stays a page. General information only; confirm against current statute before relying on any of it.

    01PC 1203.4 — dismissal
    • Probation completed, or the court exercises discretion to grant relief early
    • Not currently serving a sentence, on probation, or charged with an offense
    • The plea is withdrawn or the verdict set aside, and the case dismissed
    • Certain offenses are expressly excluded by statute
    02PC 17(b) — reduction
    • The offense must be a wobbler — chargeable as felony or misdemeanour
    • Probation must have been granted (rather than a state prison sentence)
    • Reduction is discretionary and, once granted, applies for all purposes going forward
    • There is no deadline — it can be sought years after probation ends
    03Counterintuitive points
    • Dismissal is not erasure. The entry shows as dismissed; the case does not disappear
    • 1203.4 does not restore firearm rights — that is a separate analysis with separate rules
    • Sealing and dismissal are different instruments — 851.91 is for arrests, 1203.4 for convictions
    • Automatic relief is real but incomplete — a court order is what you can actually produce

    Common questions

    Answers

    01Is expungement the same as erasing my record?

    No. Penal Code 1203.4 withdraws the plea and dismisses the case, so the entry shows as dismissed — but the case history remains. For most private employment applications that difference is decisive; for licensing and government roles, more is visible.

    02How long do I have to wait to file?

    There is no filing deadline for 17(b) reduction or 1203.4 dismissal. What usually matters is that probation has ended — and where it has not, early termination under 1203.3 can often be sought first.

    03Will this restore my gun rights?

    Not by itself. Penal Code 1203.4 expressly does not restore firearm rights, and certain convictions carry their own prohibitions under state and federal law. Firearm eligibility is a separate analysis and we keep it separate.

    04What if I went to prison rather than getting probation?

    Traditional 1203.4 relief was built around probation, but other provisions — including 1203.41 and the automatic-relief statutes — reach some sentences served in custody. Do not self-disqualify; the answer is in the docket.

    05Doesn't California clear old records automatically now?

    Partially. Automatic relief statutes clear eligible records by operation of law, but coverage is incomplete, exclusions apply and databases lag behind. A granted petition gives you a court order you can actually produce.

    06What should I bring to the first call?

    Whatever you have — and if that is nothing, a county and an approximate year is usually enough. Case numbers, probation paperwork and any prior petition documents make it faster.
